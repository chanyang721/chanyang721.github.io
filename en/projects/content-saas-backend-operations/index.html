Project Overview

I defined a commerce model where online gifts meet offline redemption requirements

The core challenge was that an AR gift was an online product and an offline experience, completed only when location, QR code, and reservation and redemption state requirements were met.

My responsibilities at the time also included implementing the main server, addressing a 3D file conversion bottleneck, and automating CI/CD. However, the clearest evidence available for public disclosure today is the Go/Gin main server's document models and router skeleton, followed by the separation of layers and infrastructure adapters in the NestJS codebase. This case study therefore focuses on location requirements, product types, AR resources, the gift redemption lifecycle, and execution boundaries rather than completed operational outcomes.

  • I represented location-based redemption requirements by separating store geofences from gift redemption coordinates and reservation times.
  • I handled AR, physical, ticket, and event products within a single gifting flow while separating their resource types.
  • Because only snapshot evidence remains, I disclose modeling and structural boundaries rather than phase-specific durations or operational metrics.

Ownership

My direct responsibilities

  • Implemented a gift lifecycle separating store geofences from QR codes and reservation, redemption, and confirmation states

Constraints

Constraints to address first

  • A gift had to be an online payment object while also carrying an offline state that allowed redemption only at a specific physical location.
  • The service also had to support gifts without a predetermined recipient and represent a flow in which the person who arrived at the coordinates could claim the gift.
  • Products such as AR resources, physical items, tickets, and events needed to share a single listing and gifting flow despite their different representations.

Phase 01 · Location & Geofence Domain

Location & Geofence Domain

Phase 01

I separated the requirement to arrive at a location before claiming a gift into explicit location conditions

I separated the store's eligible redemption area from the gift's actual redemption coordinates.

  1. 01

    Requirements Analysis

    I treated gift redemption as an experience requiring the right place, coordinates, and reservation time, rather than a simple transfer of ownership.

    • A store provided the reference point for a physical redemption area, while a gift needed its own location for placement or redemption.
    • Because gifts without predetermined recipients also had to be supported, some cases prioritized “where” over “to whom.”
  2. 02

    Domain Modeling

    Stores hold center coordinates and a radius; gifts hold redemption coordinates and a reservation time.

    • The store's location defines the geofence, while the gift's location identifies the target of the redemption event.
    • I used a precise numeric type for coordinates rather than leaving latitude and longitude loosely represented as strings or subject to floating-point error.
  3. 03

    Architecture Decisions

    I chose circular geofences over a complex spatial model to express the initial requirements as straightforward coordinate and radius checks.

    • Polygons and floor-level spaces are more expressive but increase the complexity of the initial model.
    • A circular geofence can be explained with one center and radius per store, making it suitable for initial domain validation.
  4. 04

    Implementation & Verification

    In the archived Go models, I verified that store location and radius, and gift location and reservation time, reside in separate documents.

    • Router comments for nearby-location queries and coordinate placement point to the same domain design.
    • Where handlers remain incomplete, I do not claim actual location-validation API results.
  5. 05

    Operations & Recovery

    When investigating redemption errors in operation, both the store geofence and the gift's coordinates and reservation conditions must be checked, along with the QR code.

    • Omitting location conditions allows a domain violation: a valid code used to redeem a gift outside the intended location.
    • The public page describes the location-validation strategy without exposing actual coordinates or store information.
